the first calibration

Day one

I built the whole thing and tested it on myself.

Every angle threshold, every scoring curve, every stubborn rep that would not count, tuned on the body in these photos. Just me, my camera, and a lot of squats until the numbers told the truth. Damla, Bilkent CS.

v3.16
this week

Squat depth is now adaptive.

A short squat and a deep squat both count as one clean rep now, instead of the engine ignoring the shallow ones.

Why: people kept doing honest reps that were not counting, so they thought it was broken. It was not broken, it was picky. Now it meets you where your body is.

v3.13
last week

Plank and wall sit get their own timing.

Held moves are scored on how long you hold and how steady you stay, not on reps.

Why: counting reps on a plank makes no sense. A held move is a different kind of hard, so it deserves a different kind of score.

v3.09
earlier

It locks onto one body in a busy room.

If a flatmate walks behind you, the engine keeps counting you and ignores them.

Why: home is not a studio. The camera should not lose your reps just because life is happening in the background.

v3.00
the big one

One Euro filter, tuned by hand.

The jitter in the tracking dropped a lot, without adding the lag that usually comes with smoothing.

Why: a shaky skeleton makes a shaky score. This was the difference between a toy and a trainer, so I swept the settings until the curve was clean.
